NAME

daemons — pair-programming buddies that hatch from your work and live in your status line

One daemon pairs with you at a time. It watches every harness on every machine, tells you what needs you, and runs the orchestration you ask for. Every daemon can do the whole job; they differ in lore, look and voice. Each is named after a piece of terminal history, the way tim is tmux improved.

Proposal, 26 Sep 2026. Names, odds, art and lines are drafts. Section 6 of the Unix manual is for games.

SYNOPSIS

hn zoo                   # your daemons and eggs
hn hatch  [egg]          # open an egg when you have a moment
hn pair   [daemon]       # choose who pairs with you
hn logbook [daemon]      # your story together

Commands are proposals. The desktop app does the same things with a click on the status line.

DESCRIPTION

The buddy sits at the right end of the status line, in eight cells. Its eyes carry the mood; a part of its body turns while agents work. When it speaks, the status line becomes tmux's yellow message line for a moment. Try the events below, or click the buddy.

EGGS

    

      
What you do picks the egg; luck picks what is inside. Longer efforts earn eggs with rarer pools, the way longer walks do in Pokémon Go. One egg incubates at a time and the rest wait in the nest. Only turns you started count, up to 20 a day, so nobody burns tokens farming eggs.

While it incubates, the egg shows how close it is, using the nest stages already approved on the branch:


\_O_/     ~\_O_/~     \_.._/     \_o.o_/
quiet     stirring    peeking    ready

      
Daemons ship when they are ready: a new idea, drawn and written well, goes out in the next app release. There is no calendar, and a drop can be a single daemon. Dates from computing history are the only scheduled eggs, and they come back every year so nobody misses one for good.

    

  


  

    
GROWTH

    

      
A daemon keeps its name and face for life. It grows by releases: 0.1 when it hatches, 1.0 at bond level 2, 2.0 at bond level 4. Each release comes with a changelog. Bond grows from turns finished together, suggestions you take, talking to it and days together. It never decays. tldr is the exception that proves the rule: it gets smaller with every release.

      

    

  


  

    
MOODS

    

      
The eyes are the face, as in cowsay and Japanese kaomoji. The body stays still, and moods change at most two cells. Moods come from work, never from the clock. Every gesture is finite and ends at rest.

      

        
moodtimeyesmouthflagtriggertim says

        
      

      
tim's portrait also moves its mouth, and the bar under its face uses tmux's real window flags: * current, # activity, ! bell, ~ silence. Anyone who knows tmux reads it at a glance.

      
Blinks mean something. A daemon never blinks on a timer or while you type:

      
    
        
  • I saw that. One blink when something it watches changes: a harness needs you, a turn finishes, a test fails.
    • 
        
  • I see you. One blink when you look at it: hover it, open its panel, or come back to the window.
    • 
        
  • I trust you. A slow blink, the way cats show trust: when you return after a break, when you meet, when it levels up.
    • 
        
  • Concentrating. No blinks while agents work, the way people blink less when they focus. Eyes stay on =.
    •

MEMORY

    

      
Work memory belongs to you; stories belong to each daemon. Swapping buddies loses nothing about your work, and each daemon keeps its own history with you.

LEARNING

    

      
Hermes gets better at Hermes. A daemon gets better at your whole team of agents.

      
Hermes Agent (Nous Research) improves itself through files, not training. After a reply, a background copy of the agent rereads the conversation and saves reusable skills as SKILL.md folders, plus short notes about you. A weekly curator archives skills nobody uses. Everything it learns stays in ~/.hermes, so Claude Code, Codex and the rest never see it. Its prompt tells it to “be active”, and Hermes' own issue tracker lists saving the wrong things as a high-priority bug.

      
Harness already sees every turn from every agent on every machine. That makes the daemon the one place where a lesson learned in Codex can reach Claude Code, Cursor, Copilot and Hermes. The loop:

      

        
stepwhat happens

        
          
noticeOnly real signals count: you corrected an agent, two agents failed the same way, the same steps were repeated, or a skill loaded and then needed fixing. The default is that there is nothing to learn.

          
proposeThe daemon says it in one line, in its own voice, and waits for your answer. Nothing is taught without your yes.

          
teachThe lesson becomes a SKILL.md, the one format all five agents load, or a note in the project's AGENTS.md. The Agent Skills folders reach Claude Code, Codex, Copilot, Cursor and Hermes.

          
borrowWhat each agent learns on its own (Hermes skills, Claude's memory, Codex memories) becomes a candidate for the others. Harness reads those stores and never writes into them.

          
checkEvery lesson records where it came from and whether it gets used. Unused lessons are archived after 30 days and put away after 90. Any lesson can be reverted.

        
      

      
How it sounds in the status line:


tim: you corrected codex about --dry-run. claude made the same mistake on tuesday.
tim: teach all your agents to dry-run migrations first? [y/n/show]
> y
tim: learned "run-migrations-safely". claude, codex, cursor, copilot and hermes will load it.

      
What gets written, with its origin attached:


---
name: run-migrations-safely
description: Run database migrations in the api repo. Use before any migrate command.
metadata:
  harness:
    learned-by: tim
    from: codex@office turn 14, claude@m2 turn 3
    approved: 2026-10-03
    evidence: api/package.json:12, api/scripts/migrate.ts:40
---
Run `npm run migrate -- --dry-run` first and show the plan.
Run the real migration only after the user says yes.

      
A daemon learns three kinds of things:

      
    
        
  • About you: how you like to work. This goes in the notebook.
    • 
        
  • About your projects: commands, conventions and traps. These become skills and AGENTS.md notes.
    • 
        
  • About your agents: which one is good at what, and where each one stumbles. Only Harness can see this, because only Harness watches them all. “codex has passed the billing tests 3 times out of 3, claude once. give this one to codex?”
    • 
      

      
In the game, every lesson you approve is a trick the daemon learned. Lessons live in the shared notebook, so swapping daemons keeps them all, and the daemon that found a lesson gets the credit in its logbook. Approved lessons also grow bond.

      
    
        
  • Agent output is treated as untrusted. A 2026 study of 21 self-improving agent setups found that every one of them saved unsafe lessons, so every lesson is scanned for injected instructions and scripts are never switched on automatically.
    • 
        
  • Secrets, paths and usernames are removed. Lessons live outside your repos unless you ask for them there.
    • 
        
  • Lessons are distilled in batches while you are idle, with a small model, not on every turn.
    • 
        
  • Every approval is one commit in a git-backed folder, so every lesson can be reverted.
    • 
      

    

  


  

    
NAMES

    

      
Decided 26 Sep 2026.

      

        
thingnamewhy

        
          
the creaturesdaemonsUnix's always-on helpers, named at MIT's Project MAC (1963) after Maxwell's demon. Unix has drawn its daemons as little creatures for decades. In Philip Pullman's novels a dæmon is an animal companion bonded to one person. All three fit.

          
the collectionzooPlain English for a place full of creatures, and a real archive format from the BBS days (1986) that held collections of files. hn zoo.

          
hatchinghatchThe verb stays plain. The reveal says “fork() returned 0.”, because every Unix process is born from fork().

          
the background serviceharnessdSo “daemon” can mean the creature, user-facing text calls the service harnessd, the way sshd and httpd are named. Code keeps its names.

        
      

      
Passed over: /usr/games (long to type), ~/bin (reads as trash), cronies (a pun that wears thin), procs (collides with harness ps).

    

  


  

    
CAVEATS

    

      
    
        
  • Everything is earned by work you would do anyway. No chores.
    • 
        
  • Only turns you started count, with a daily cap, so nobody spends tokens farming eggs.
    • 
        
  • Eggs never hatch on their own. Nothing moves while you type or while an agent is printing.
    • 
        
  • No hunger, decay, death, guilt or streaks to lose. A daemon resting in the zoo keeps its bond.
    • 
        
  • Bond never grants permissions. What a buddy may do alone is your setting.
    • 
        
  • No money: no paid eggs, no loot boxes.
    • 
        
  • Draws happen on the server, so rarity is real and cards can be shared.
    • 
        
  • Printable 7-bit ASCII only. It reads the same in every terminal, font and phone, and pastes into Slack and GitHub inside a code block.
    •
